QUESTION B.3 · THERMODYNAMIC TRIAD 3 Weather Regimes

What patterns can be observed between radiation, temperature, and humidity?

Radiation, Temperature, and Humidity form a coupled three-state atmospheric system. While Temperature and Radiation rise together (r = +0.735), Humidity acts as a cloud-cover gate (r = -0.226 with radiation; r = -0.286 with temperature):

  • 1. Clear-Sky Harvest (Temp >56°F, RH <65%): Smooth bell curve, zero cloud attenuation, sustained 950–1,085 W/m² midday plateau.
  • 2. Overcast Suppression (Temp <50°F, RH >85%): Stratus clouds block direct beam flux; midday radiation drops below 200 W/m² (165 midday readings <100 W/m²).
  • 3. Broken-Cloud Lensing (Temp 58–63°F, RH 85–98%): Scattered cumulus clouds produce violent oscillations between 400 W/m² cloud shadows and 1,350–1,601 W/m² edge-scattering spikes.
QUESTION B.4 · OUTLIERS & ANOMALIES 49 Spikes > 1,200 W/m²

Are there any unusual observations or outliers? What might explain them?

Yes—three physically distinct outlier classes appear in the Voyage-123 logs:

  • • Super-Solar Constant Spikes (Max 1,601.26 W/m²): 49 readings exceed 1,200 W/m² and 6 exceed Earth’s TOA Solar Constant (~1,361 W/m²). Caused by Cloud-Edge Enhancement (Mie Scattering) where direct sunlight + diffuse reflection from cumulus cloud walls focus onto the sensor.
  • • Gale-Force Wind Outliers (Max 40.50 mph): Mean wind speed is 6.24 mph (IQR 3.37–7.87 mph), but winter low-pressure fronts trigger gusts up to 40.50 mph (+9.8σ).
  • • Nighttime Non-Zero Baseline (1.11–4.64 W/m²) & RH >100%: Thermopile pyranometer infrared dome offset at night (~1.23 W/m²) and capacitive hygrometer supersaturation (101–103% RH) during dense fog.
PART B BONUS · NON-OBVIOUS INSIGHTS & QUANTITATIVE PROOF Critical Strategic Value for Rebellion Base Grid

Insight 1: The Peak-vs.-Average Day Paradox (Zero Overlap!)

Cross-referencing the Top 5 days by instantaneous peak radiation against the Top 5 days by daily average radiation reveals zero overlap. The #1 peak day (2016-09-04, max 1,601.26 W/m²) had a mean humidity of 93.88% and a daily average radiation of just 153.58 W/m² (ranking 84th out of 118 days!). Conversely, the #1 daily energy day (2016-09-19, mean 335.50 W/m²) had 43.09% humidity and a smooth peak of 1,085.75 W/m².

→ Engineering Takeaway: High-humidity broken-cloud days trigger dangerous inverter surge spikes (>1,400 W/m²) while delivering <50% of the daily battery charge of dry clear-sky days!

Insight 2: Afternoon Orographic Cloud Asymmetry & Thermal Lag

Although 09:00 and 15:00 are symmetrically spaced around solar noon (~12:15), afternoon radiation at 15:00 (370.72 W/m²) is 32.7% lower than morning radiation at 09:00 (550.66 W/m²). Why? Daytime solar heating drives upslope valley breezes (WindDirection shifts from 180° to 45°), lifting moist air that condenses into afternoon cloud banks (mean RH jumps from 69.1% at 09:00 to 78.8% at 15:00).

→ Engineering Takeaway: Schedule heavy base battery charging between 08:30 and 12:30 HST before afternoon upslope cloud attenuation sets in.

FEATURE USEFULNESS ANALYSIS

Which Features Predict Solar Radiation Best?

1. SolarElevationProxy (62.6% RF / 41.0% GBDT): sin(π·(t - t_rise)/DayLength) captures the non-linear solar zenith bell curve and zeros out nighttime intervals.

3. TimeDecimal, DayOfYear & DayLengthHours: Capture the 32.7% afternoon cloud asymmetry and autumn-to-winter solar declination decay.
